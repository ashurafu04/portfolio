# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Enterprise decision-makers & hiring managers** — the audience is dual but converges on the same need: evaluating whether Achraf is the right person to trust with high-stakes engineering problems.

- **Enterprise clients** (CTOs, technical directors, procurement leads): evaluating a consultant for serious architectural work — AI orchestration, headless commerce, cloud infrastructure. They're scanning for credibility, track record, and depth. They don't want to be impressed by a portfolio; they want to be convinced.
- **Recruiters & engineering managers**: screening for senior backend / AI engineering roles. They're pattern-matching for signal over noise — concrete skills, real projects, clear positioning.

Both audiences share the same threshold: "Can I bet a real project on this person?"

## Product Purpose

Achraf Malki's professional portfolio. A single-page site that communicates engineering expertise and establishes the case for trust with enterprise clients and employers.

Success looks like: a visitor who arrived skeptical leaves confident. The design and content together compress the due-diligence work — positioning, evidence, contact — into a single, authoritative session.

## Brand Personality

**Precise · Ambitious · Uncompromising**

Voice: direct, expert, no hedging. The writing speaks in outcomes and systems — "95% operational autonomy", "Zero-PII compliance", "doesn't break under pressure". The design must match that register. Every visual choice should feel deliberate, not decorative.

Emotional goal: **confident trust** — "This person can handle serious problems. I'd bet my project on them."

## Anti-references

What this should NOT look like:

- **Generic AI-generated portfolio**: cream/sand/warm-neutral body, eyebrow labels over every section, identical icon-card grids, gradient text accents. The saturated 2026 AI aesthetic is the opposite of "uncompromising."
- **Creative agency showcase**: flashy scroll-jacking, experimental layout, visual spectacle at the expense of clarity. This is an engineer, not a creative director.
- **Personal developer blog minimalism**: thin type hierarchy, no visual weight, feels like a GitHub README. Doesn't signal enterprise scale.
- **SaaS hero-metric template**: big numbers, colored stat grids, gradient accents — telegraphs startup hype, not systems credibility.
- **Freelancer portfolio excitement**: neon accents, animated gradients, "hire me!" energy. The work sells itself; the design's job is to get out of the way without disappearing.

## Design Principles

1. **Architectural restraint as signal** — visual decisions should feel like engineering decisions: purposeful, load-bearing, no redundant elements. If a decoration doesn't add information or hierarchy, it doesn't belong.
2. **Let the work carry the argument** — project descriptions and skill depth are the proof. Design frames them without competing. Copy drives; layout follows.
3. **Enterprise trust over developer flair** — choices should read as reliable and serious, not as a creative coding experiment. Calibrate every decision against: "Does this make a CTO more or less likely to email me?"
4. **Ambition expressed through precision** — boldness is welcome, but it should land as mastery, not decoration. A striking typographic scale is ambition. A gradient rainbow is noise.
5. **Both audiences, one message** — a recruiter and a CTO should read the same thing: exceptional, serious, capable. No content or design choice should speak to one at the cost of the other.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance as the baseline:
- Minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio for body text, 3:1 for large text and UI components
- Keyboard navigable throughout
- Screen reader compatible (semantic HTML, ARIA where needed)
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected — all animations must have a crossfade / instant fallback
- No color-only information encoding
